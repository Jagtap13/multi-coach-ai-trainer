General Nutrition Principles for Fitness Goals

Macronutrients
Protein (4 kcal/g) supports muscle repair and growth; general recommendations range from 1.6-2.2g/kg bodyweight for active individuals. Carbohydrates (4 kcal/g) are the primary fuel source for high-intensity training. Fats (9 kcal/g) support hormone production, with 0.5-1g/kg bodyweight as a reasonable baseline.

Caloric Balance
Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) is the sum of basal metabolic rate, activity level, and the thermic effect of food. Weight change is driven by the relationship between caloric intake and TDEE: a surplus supports muscle gain, a deficit supports fat loss, and maintenance supports body recomposition or performance maintenance.

Meal Timing and Frequency
While total daily intake matters most for body composition outcomes, distributing protein intake across 3-5 meals (roughly 0.4g/kg per meal) may optimize muscle protein synthesis throughout the day.

Hydration
Adequate hydration (roughly 30-40ml per kg bodyweight daily, more with intense training) supports performance, recovery, and metabolic function.

Micronutrients
A varied diet including sufficient fruits, vegetables, and whole foods helps meet vitamin and mineral needs that support recovery, immune function, and long-term health, which indirectly supports training consistency.

Diet Adherence
The most effective nutrition plan is one that can be sustained long-term. Extreme restriction often leads to poor adherence and rebound weight gain; moderate, consistent approaches tend to produce better long-term results.

Allergy and Intolerance-Friendly Substitutions

When a user reports a food allergy or intolerance, prioritize substitutions that preserve the same macronutrient role rather than simply removing the food. For lactose intolerance or dairy allergy, replace dairy protein sources with options such as eggs, poultry, fish, tofu, tempeh, legumes, or plant-based protein powders; note that many "yogurt" or "milk" alternatives made from coconut, soy, oat, or rice are dairy-free by definition and safe to recommend.

For peanut allergy, avoid peanuts and peanut butter specifically, and substitute with other nut butters (almond, cashew, sunflower seed butter for tree-nut-safe cases) or seed-based spreads. For a broader tree nut allergy, avoid all nut-based substitutions and favor seed-based or dairy-based alternatives instead.

For gluten intolerance or celiac disease, replace wheat-based carbohydrate sources (bread, pasta, regular oats) with gluten-free grains such as rice, quinoa, certified gluten-free oats, or potatoes.

For shellfish or egg allergies, substitute with other lean protein sources such as poultry, fish (for shellfish allergy only, if fish itself is tolerated), legumes, or dairy-based proteins where appropriate.

As a general principle, always substitute a flagged ingredient with an alternative from the same macronutrient category, so the overall meal plan's protein, carbohydrate, and fat targets remain consistent even after the substitution.
