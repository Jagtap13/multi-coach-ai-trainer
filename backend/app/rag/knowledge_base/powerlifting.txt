Strength Training and Powerlifting Principles

Powerlifting focuses on maximizing performance in three competition lifts: the squat, bench press, and deadlift. Strength development differs from hypertrophy training in its emphasis on neural adaptation and force production at high intensities.

Intensity and Rep Ranges
Strength-focused training typically uses lower rep ranges (1-6 reps) at higher percentages of one-rep max (80-95% of 1RM). This range prioritizes neuromuscular efficiency and skill in the specific lift pattern.

Periodization
Linear periodization (gradually increasing intensity while decreasing volume over a training block) and undulating periodization (varying intensity and volume within a week) are both effective. Peaking blocks before competition typically reduce volume significantly while maintaining or increasing intensity.

Specificity and Technique
Because powerlifting is judged on a fixed movement pattern, technique practice at competition-specific tempos and depths is essential. Accessory work should address individual weak points identified through movement analysis (e.g., weak lockout, sticking point off the chest).

Rest Periods
Strength training requires longer rest periods, typically 3-5 minutes between heavy sets, to allow adequate recovery of the phosphagen energy system for near-maximal effort.

Recovery and Fatigue Management
Because near-maximal loading creates high central nervous system fatigue, deload weeks (reduced volume/intensity every 4-6 weeks) help sustain long-term progress and reduce injury risk.
