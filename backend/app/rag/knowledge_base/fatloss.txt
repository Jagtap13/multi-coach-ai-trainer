Fat Loss and Body Composition Principles

Fat loss fundamentally requires a sustained caloric deficit — consuming fewer calories than the body expends. However, training and nutrition strategy significantly affect how much of the weight lost comes from fat versus lean mass.

Caloric Deficit
A moderate deficit of 15-25% below maintenance calories is generally recommended for sustainable fat loss while minimizing muscle loss, compared to aggressive deficits which increase risk of losing lean mass and metabolic adaptation.

Resistance Training During a Deficit
Maintaining resistance training during a caloric deficit is critical to preserve muscle mass. Training volume can be reduced slightly compared to a muscle-building phase, but intensity (weight on the bar) should be maintained as much as possible to signal the body to retain muscle.

Cardio and NEAT
Both structured cardiovascular exercise and Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT — daily movement outside of formal exercise) contribute to total energy expenditure. Increasing NEAT (e.g., step count) is often a more sustainable lever than adding large amounts of cardio.

Protein Intake
Higher protein intake (1.6-2.2g per kg of bodyweight) during a caloric deficit helps preserve lean mass and supports satiety, making adherence to the deficit easier.

Rate of Loss
A sustainable rate of fat loss is approximately 0.5-1% of body weight per week. Faster rates increase the risk of muscle loss, metabolic slowdown, and poor long-term adherence.
